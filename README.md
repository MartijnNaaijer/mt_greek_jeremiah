# Stipp's *Textkritische Synopse zum Jeremiabuch* as HTML

One page per chapter of Jeremiah, plus the parallel at 2 Kings 25, setting the
masoretic text beside the retroverted alexandrian text and Stipp's own
unretroverted Greek.

Source: Hermann-Josef Stipp, *Textkritische Synopse zum Jeremiabuch*, 15. Aufl.,
April 2021.

Read it here: **https://martijnnaaijer.github.io/mt_greek_jeremiah/**

## What is here

    docs/index.html      the chapter index — start here
    docs/jer01.html …    Jeremiah 1–52, one page per chapter
    docs/2kings25.html   the three Synopse pages of the parallel

54 files. Each is self-contained: the data is embedded, the CSS is inline,
nothing is fetched, so a page can be opened from the file system, mailed to
someone, or served as it stands.

The folder is called `docs/` because that is the only subfolder a GitHub Pages
branch deployment can serve, and it is served at the site root — so
`docs/jer02.html` is `…github.io/mt_greek_jeremiah/jer02.html`. The name is
GitHub's convention, not a description of the contents.

## What is on a page

Each verse is a block; each clause is a row of three columns — masoretic,
alexandrian (retroverted), and Stipp's Greek at the right — with his clause
letter between the two Hebrew columns and his apparatus beneath. Colour marks
what the two editions do not share: masoretic plus, alexandrian plus, the two
readings of a qualitative variant, and a stretch Stipp brackets without giving
an alternative.

Every Hebrew and Greek word carries a hover with its analysis. A masoretic word
is matched to the BHSA words of its own verse — 98.3% of them are, since the
repair put the column into BHSA's order; an alexandrian word has no node
to link to — it is a retroversion of the Greek — so it falls back to an index of
forms with exactly one analysis anywhere in BHSA, and the hover says so. The
Greek is analysed from the Rahlfs LXX in Text-Fabric, lemma in Greek script.
Ketiv/qere is marked with a superscript K, in the masoretic column only.

## How far to trust it

These pages are generated by extraction from a PDF. The masoretic column is the
only one with a ground truth, and it is both checked and **repaired** against
BHSA; the other two are not. Every verse carries a badge saying which it is.

- **The masoretic column is repaired against BHSA.** 893 of the 1,394 verses
  need nothing and carry `BHSA ✓`. The other 501 carry `BHSA ✎ +n −n`: **1,478
  words supplied** from BHSA and **569 dropped** that BHSA does not have in that
  verse. A supplied word is set in its own colour and in BHSA's pointing, so it
  is visibly the database's word and not Stipp's print. After the repair
  **1,385 of 1,394 verses reproduce BHSA's consonants exactly** (the nine others
  print the qere, which BHSA also holds); before it, 899 did.
- **Sixteen verses that the extraction lost entirely** — 13:10, 16:6, 17:2–5,
  22:12, 22:27, 23:10, 23:34, 27:14, 28:8–9, 33:11, 44:16, 52:20 — are present
  and wholly supplied. They have no alexandrian column and no Greek, because
  nothing was extracted for them to carry.
- **What the repair cannot do:** it works one verse at a time and never moves a
  word across a verse boundary. A word the extraction put in the neighbouring
  verse is dropped there and supplied here, so the text of both verses is right,
  but Stipp's colour on that word is lost. Jer 1:15/16 is the standard case.
- The BWHEBB→Unicode map agrees with BHSA on **97.9%** of words; the Greek map,
  checked against Rahlfs, gives **96.6%** attested forms.
- The Greek is placed in the right Rahlfs verse for **90.2%** of its words.
  Where the clause counts of the two columns agree — 1,062 verses of 1,246 — the
  verse is marked `Gr ✓`; where they do not, the Greek has still been dealt out
  in order and the verse is marked `Gr ≈`.
- Greek versification is Greek: the LXX Jer 32 is the Hebrew Jer 25. A verse
  number in the Greek panel is not an MT verse number.

## Where it comes from

Output only. Nothing here is hand-edited, and nothing in the pipeline reads back
from it. The pages are written by

    deuteronomistic/scripts/build_synopse_pages.py

in the parent project, from `results/synopse.json` (`parse_synopse.py`), with
`check_synopse.py` supplying the per-verse badge, `bhsa_lex.py` the Hebrew
analyses and `lxx_greek.py` the Greek. To change a page, change the script and
re-run it; the whole folder is regenerated in one pass.
