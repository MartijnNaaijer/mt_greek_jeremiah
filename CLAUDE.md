# Stipp's Synopse as readable text

This repository turns Hermann-Josef Stipp, *Textkritische Synopse zum
Jeremiabuch*, **15. korrigierte interne Auflage**, April 2021, into 55 HTML pages:
the masoretic text, Stipp's Hebrew retroversion of the alexandrian (Old Greek)
text, and his Greek, sentence by sentence, with a hover analysis of every word
from BHSA and the Rahlfs LXX. Cite the source with that designation wherever the
title appears — in the pages, in the README and here.

It sits inside a larger research project on the language of Jeremiah
(`deuteronomistic/` of `dating_dtr_redaction_jeremiah_claude`), whose own
CLAUDE.md carries the same record. **This file is the record for this
repository**; the project's copy of these sections is the older one.

```
docs/        the 55 pages (output only; GitHub Pages serves /docs at the site root)
scripts/     the code that builds them, with its own paths.py
tests/       the suite over the built pages, and baseline.json
README.md    the public description and reliability figures
```

Live at **https://martijnnaaijer.github.io/mt_greek_jeremiah/**, repository
https://github.com/MartijnNaaijer/mt_greek_jeremiah. **The repository is public.**

## What is not here, and never will be

- **Stipp's PDF** (`StippJer_Textkritische_Synopse_2021.pdf`) and its Einleitung
  (`StippJer_Textkritische_Synopse_2021_Einleitun.pdf`, whose Zeichenerklärung is
  on its last two pages and whose worked example on p. 5 is Jer 1,1a). It is an
  internal edition: **never commit it**, nor a rendered crop of it. `paths.BOOKS()`
  finds it in the research project at `deuteronomistic/paper/books_for paper/`, or
  in `SYNOPSE_BOOKS`.
- **The intermediate files** — `synopse.json`, the BHSA and LXX word lists,
  `synopse_check.json`, `mt_vs_bhsa.csv`. `paths.R()` keeps them in the research
  project's `deuteronomistic/results/`, or in `SYNOPSE_RESULTS`, because
  `deuteronomistic/scripts/bracket_crosscheck.py` there also reads `synopse.json`
  (and imports `bwfonts` from this `scripts/` folder).
- **BHSA 2021 and the Rahlfs LXX**, read through text-fabric from
  `~/text-fabric-data` (`etcbc/bhsa/tf/2021`, `CenterBLC/lxx/tf/1935`).

Every path is built by `scripts/paths.py`, absolute and keyed off `__file__`:
`R()` an intermediate file, `BOOKS()` the PDF, `MTG()` a page in `docs/`. No
script opens a bare filename.

## Build

Run from `scripts/`, always with `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8` (Hebrew crashes the
cp1252 console otherwise):

    lxx_greek.py            Rahlfs LXX Jeremiah -> word list and form index
    bhsa_lex.py             BHSA -> per-verse words and form index (the hover)
    parse_synopse.py        Stipp's PDF -> results/synopse.json, one record per sentence
    check_synopse.py        the masoretic column against BHSA -> the per-verse badge
    build_synopse_pages.py  -> docs/, all 55 pages in one pass

Diagnostics, not part of the build: `pages_vs_bhsa.py` (every deviation of the
built masoretic column from BHSA, consonant by consonant, to
`results/mt_vs_bhsa.csv`, with a guess at where each missing run went — its
categories are a hypothesis, not a measurement) and `notes_vs_pdf.py` (the notes
against the PDF read independently with PyMuPDF). `bwfonts.py` holds the frozen
converters for Stipp's BWHEBB and BWGRKL fonts (`python bwfonts.py` runs the
Hebrew map's own check, 82.30% on a strict byte-for-byte test);
`bwhebb_learn.py` derived the Hebrew map and is kept as the record of how.

**Tests**: from the repository root, `python -m unittest discover -s tests`,
standard library only. **102 tests** (87 of them without BHSA). Sixty-two exist
because of a numbered fault below — one per fault, on the verse that showed it —
and the rest are floors and ceilings in `tests/baseline.json`, whose `_` key
records for every figure what it was and why it moved. **A floor that moves must
be explained there, in the same change.** Jer 1,1 and 28,1 are each pinned end to
end. The test helper's `mt_words()` returns SPANS, not words; a word-division
test must read `mt_printed()`.

**The pages are output only.** Nothing in `docs/` is hand-edited; to change a
page, change a script and rebuild. The German wording (badge titles, subtitles,
the overview's note) lives in `build_synopse_pages.py` alone, and every figure
either index prints is computed there at the point of writing — never transcribed.

**Two indexes, since 2026-10-01, on the author's decision.** `index.html`, the
site's main page, is an ENGLISH introduction written by `english_index()`: what
Stipp's synopsis is, why the two editions of Jeremiah matter, how to read a page,
how far it can be trusted, and the author's credit for the conversion. The German
overview with its notes on what is checked is `de.html`. Every chapter page links
to both (`Start`, `Übersicht`). The English text uses no em dash, on the author's
instruction; keep it that way.

## Working rules

- **BHSA is the check, never the source.** A repair that spliced BHSA's words into
  the masoretic column was built and removed on 2026-09-05, on the author's
  objection: at Jer 1,1 it marked מן הכהנים as supplied from the database when
  Stipp prints it plainly. **The text is all in the Synopse; the work is to read
  it correctly.** Letters may be completed only from Stipp's own page (26)–(27),
  and only where BHSA then confirms them.
- **Named lists need a crop and the author's word.** `parse_synopse.WIDE_BARS` (5
  entries), `parse_synopse.APPARATUS_ONLY` (1) and
  `build_synopse_pages.SOURCE_DIFFS` (5) each record a decision taken by the
  author on 2026-10-01 against a rendered crop of the PDF. Do not add an entry by
  inference.
- **Print the tokens.** Every fault below was found by reading verses, sorted by
  the size of their deviation, and asking what the cases have in common — not by
  a summary figure. A column with no ground truth (the alexandrian, the notes)
  accumulates faults silently; look there on purpose.
- **Compare against a run of the SAME code.** Save `synopse.json` before a change
  and diff the records after it, text and notes separately; a change that should
  touch notes only must leave every text column byte-identical. A new test must
  fail on the old pages before it is believed.
- **Re-sweep thresholds after each fix.** A threshold swept against a metric that
  another fault is corrupting is set in the wrong place (`OVERFLOW_X`, below).
- **Edit regex code with the Edit tool, never through a shell heredoc.** Heredocs
  on this machine have turned `\1` into a control character, `\b` into a
  backspace, and swallowed line continuations — four times. Read the line back.
- **PyMuPDF is installed** (`import pymupdf`): glyph-by-glyph positions with real
  boxes, and `page.get_pixmap(clip=..., dpi=300)` renders a crop of the Synopse to
  put beside a built page — which is how every case-by-case decision was made.

## Text-fabric traps that cost a verse each

- **`T.sectionFromNode` returns `2_Kings`, not `Kings_II`.** That name matched
  nothing until 2026-09-05, and 2 Kings 25 silently had no hovers, no badges and no
  check. The corpus is 1,394 verses (1,364 of Jeremiah and 30 of 2 Kings 25).
- **BHSA's `book` feature gives Latin names** (`Jeremia`, `Reges_II`) and lives on
  book NODES: `F.book.v(word)` is `None`. Same trap for `chapter` in the LXX set —
  `F.chapter.v(verse_node)` is `None`, and LXX Jeremiah came out as 62 verses.
- **BHSA `lex` nodes are not unique per lexeme string** (the article and the
  interrogative are both `H`); keep the most frequent node per string.
- **The LXX is in Greek versification**: its Jer 32 is MT Jer 25. Stipp's page
  headings give the Greek range (`Jer 25,11–22 / Jer G 32,1–8`), and since (60)
  the panel's own `Jer G n` headers give the chapter of each Greek line.

## Terminology

**Stipp's lettered segments are SENTENCES, not cola** — *Sätze* in German, and
*sentence* in English, everywhere. The author's instruction (2026-09-09 and
-11): a colon is part of a stichus, and Hebrew PROSE has no stichometry and so no
cola. **The parser's identifiers still say `clause`** (`read_clause`,
`clauses_of`) — the same unit under an older name; renaming them is a refactor
not yet done.

**A label with a subscript index — a₁ a₂ a₃ — marks the parts of ONE sentence**
split by an embedded sentence, and counts once (the author's instruction; it
also confirms (5,23) below). Records with no Hebrew, only a note, are not
sentences. The book has **5,004** sentences; the count lives in
`build_synopse_pages.sentences()`. Twelve verses repeat a letter with NO index —
where a raised P or D rides on the label and one sentence is set in two pieces —
and they collapse too.

**word** means a BHSA word (the ETCBC splits prefixed article, preposition,
conjunction; not suffixes). **graphical unit** means what is written between
spaces, maqqef counting as a break. Never write "morpheme".

## The record of faults

Numbered in the order they were found. Each is recorded at the code too. The
faults that describe pypdf's output stay here after the code that compensated
for them was removed, because a future reader of pypdf will meet them again.

### Reading the notation

Stipp's Einleitung states most of this; each line cost a fault to find. (1) **§ opens a notation and # closes it** — "§ markiert den Beginn und # das Ende" — not the other way round, which is what the character stream suggests, since BWHEBB runs left to right and # is met first. (2) **A clause begins at its label, not ends at it**: the label sits at the right edge, which in a right-to-left column is the start, so Jer 1,1a runs over three printed lines with the label only on the first. (3) **A bare backslash refers to one word on each side** — "den kleinstmöglichen Verweisrahmen", maqqef counting as a space — and only a § … # notation extends that reach; reading it as dividing the whole clause gave Jer 1,2 four masoretic words and handed the rest to the alexandrian column.

(5, 23) **The small digit in a label is a Klammerkonstruktion index, not a verse number**, set at 6.36pt beside a label at 9.88pt — and reading it as a verse number lost 61 verses at 4,23a. But it is set as its OWN SPAN, and `label_span()` returns the last labelish span at the right edge, so refusing it for its size threw the letter away with it: 28,1 printed blanks where Stipp prints b₁ and b₂. `label_spans()` collects them all — the full-size ones give the letter and the verse number, the small one goes back onto the letter as its index. Clauses carrying text with no letter: **394 → 134**; 256 now show an index (`a2` 73, `a1` 70, `b1` 29, `b2` 28 …).

(22) **A raised siglum can ride on the label.** Stipp raises a P for Parablepsis and a D for Dittographie on the sentence they qualify and sets it inside the label span — `] aP 10` at 13,9 — and rejecting the label for the capital letter is where **the eight missing verses went**: 13,10; 22,12; 22,27; 23,34; 28,8–9; 44,16; 52,20, each running on into the verse before (13,9 carried 100 letters of 13,10). The siglum is allowed **only when a letter or a number is there too**: of the 52 right-edge spans a looser test admits, 12 are real labels carrying a sign and 40 are the sign alone, a bare `P` (33) or `D` (7) that would open a clause where there is none.

(25) **A plus does not nest.** A bracket running over a line break is RE-OPENED at the head of the next line, so a clause carries two `]` and one `[`. Counted as a depth that made 2, the single `[` brought it back to 1 and the plus never closed: 28,1a kept `וַיְהִי` alone in the alexandrian column while the Greek there has the whole date. **45 clauses ended with a plus still open.** `mt` and `og` are binary, not counters.

(17) **A `>` in a margin reference is the siglum for "fehlt in", not a bracket.** `7,30–8,3 > 4Q70* (4QJer a*)` at Jer 8,1, read as a bracket, opened a plus that never closed and sent the whole of 8,1b to the alexandrian column — **89 letters, the largest single loss in the book**. Markup and siglum are told apart by position, since the margin is set left to right while the column is not: real markup is the leftmost thing on the printed line, so nothing but space follows it in its span. Of the 636 bracket characters in margin spans, 623 end their span and 13 do not.

(26) **Where the two forms differ only in their first letter, the shared letters are printed once — on the alexandrian side.** Stipp sets the masoretic head, the bar, and the alexandrian word IN FULL, and the reader carries his eye across the bar to finish the word. At 52,9 the line is `דִּבְלָתָה\רִ` and the masoretic reading is רִבְלָתָה; at 23,20b it is `בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים תִּ\יִתְבּוֹנְנוּ בָהּ` and the masoretic word is תִּתְבּוֹנְנוּ; 3,23 `מִהָ` against `הַגְּבָעוֹת הֲמוֹן` is it twice inside one word. **Sixteen verses were short for this reason alone**, and until 2026-09-09 all sixteen were left as they stood. **That decision is reversed, and the reason it was wrong is worth keeping**: it read the abbreviation as a statement about the column when it is a saving of ink. The letters come from Stipp's page and from nowhere else — the repair built and removed on 2026-09-05 took them from BHSA, which is a different thing and still forbidden. The sixteen are exact against BHSA, as is the seventeenth that (27) adds, the count of verses carrying letters BHSA does not have **did not move**, and the class is carried as `mtsup` so no count can mistake it for something Stipp sets twice.

**The setting is what marks the class, not the reading.** A bar between two whole words is set with a space on each side; a bar inside one word is set hard against the text on both sides — 66 of the book's 820. Tightness alone is not enough, because the same setting is used when the ALEXANDRIAN form is the abbreviated one (`מַרְעִיתִי\תָם` at 23,1, where the masoretic word is already whole) and for short whole-word variants bound by maqqef. Three tests together pick out the seventeen places, and each was measured against all 66: the masoretic piece has **fewer consonants** than the alexandrian one; it **does not end in a maqqef**, which is a word boundary in Stipp's own terms; and it **cannot stand as a word**, ending on a pointed letter or being a single letter. The four ways a word CAN end on a pointed letter are named rather than discovered — a final form, a mappiq in ה, a furtive patah under ח or ע — since `בָּהּ` at 8,19 is a whole word and `הָ` at 3,23 is half of one. **One letter is dropped from the alexandrian word, not two**, except where the masoretic head closes on a silent sheva: at 5,18 `אִתְּ\אֶתְכֶם` gives אִתְּכֶם and not אִתְּתְכֶם, because a letter under a sheva cannot follow one. The test is on the POINTS and not on the letters, which is what keeps 23,20 out of it — its taw carries a hiriq, its syllable is open, and the taw does repeat.

(27) **A bar inside a word can sit in a `§ … #` notation instead of standing bare, and then the whole of the alexandrian side completes the head.** A bare bar reaches one word on each side — Stipp's *kleinstmöglicher Verweisrahmen* — so where the letters the two forms share run over more than one word he has to state the reach. **7,9 is the only place in the book.** He sets `הֲ\וְגָנֹב * <וְ>רָצֹחַ וְנָאֹף`, and the masoretic reading is הֲגָנֹב רָצֹחַ וְנָאֹף: the alexandrian side entire, less its own first letter and less the וְ his brackets already mark as alexandrian. The star is his sign for the transposition — the Greek has the three verbs in another order, murder, adultery, theft. The masoretic column had `הֲ` and was short **ten letters**, the largest of the abbreviation losses.

**All seventeen are closed.** Sixteen by the bare-bar rule and 7,9 by (27); 2,25 was completed here and its remaining loss was a different fault, closed by (28). Every one of the seventeen now agrees with BHSA exactly.

(30) **A notation can close on the NEXT SENTENCE, and the reach it states is Stipp's, not his sentence division's.** At 6,6 the `§` rides on the label of sentence d, the bar stands in sentence e and the `#` at its left edge. Read sentence by sentence the notation never opened, so the bar was taken as bare, reached one word on each side, and the surplus of the **alexandrian** reading stayed `common` — printed in BOTH columns. The masoretic column of 6,6 read הִיא הָעִיר הָפְקַד **עִיר הַשֶּׁקֶר**, where BHS has only the first three words and the last two are the Greek's ὦ πόλις ψευδής. **Five verses were wrong that way.** Four printed an alexandrian reading as masoretic text — 6,6, 20,11, 33,2, 44,12 — and 22,4 went the other way for the same reason, losing הוּא וַעֲבָדָו וְעַמּוֹ into the alexandrian column, twelve letters BHS has. Exact against BHSA 1,339 → **1,344**, word for word 1,274 → **1,279**, extra-text ceiling 23 → **19**; nothing else moved. `read_clause()` takes a `carry` and hands one on; the bracket depths are not carried, because a plus is re-opened at the head of every line.

**This is the class of fault these pages exist to prevent, and no figure here names it.** The badge counts consonants, so an alexandrian word standing in the masoretic column reads as "extra text" and nothing says where it came from. That is why CLAUDE.md and the README both said until 2026-09-10 that the extra text was **Stipp's footnote apparatus read as text**. It was not, and the apparatus accounts for hardly any of it: of the 20 verses whose only fault was extra text, **17** carried the run in a segment classed `common` that the alexandrian column also holds. The lesson is (4)'s — **print the tokens** — applied to the diagnostic's own categories: sort the deviations by size, then ask of each run not only where it is missing from but which column it came out of.

**The reach is bounded by a LOOKAHEAD, not by a threshold, and that distinction is the whole of the fix.** A notation is handed on only if the sentence that follows puts a bar in it before closing it (`bar_inside()`). Stipp has two notations and only the barred one may reach: a `§ … #` with no bar is his marked stretch, where the two editions have the same consonants and the difference is described in the note. Carried into that, the whole of the next sentence goes to the masoretic column and **the alexandrian cell empties** — 48,38 sets אֵין־חֵפֶץ בּוֹ, a transposition star and נְאֻם־יְהוָה inside one such notation, and the Greek has all of it in another order. Two thresholds were tried first and both measured **inert** once the lookahead was in: a ceiling on sentence boundaries crossed, and a refusal to hand a notation on from a sentence that has already resolved a bare bar. With the lookahead only five sentences in the book inherit a notation at all and none inherits it twice. **What follows the bar does not carry either** — that guard is real and worth 33,6 and 48,9.

**21,8c is the one place in the book where Stipp sets the pair the other way round**, and it is NOT repaired. Reading right to left the `#` rides on the label, the bar follows, and the `§` comes after it at x = 187: the pair brackets the bar, but the opener is met last. The bar is therefore read as bare and the trailing `§` opens a notation with no close anywhere — carried, it ran nine sentences to 21,10c and emptied the alexandrian cell of every one. The lookahead stops it. The verse still prints אָנִי נָתַתִּי in the masoretic column beside הִנְנִי נֹתֵן, seven letters BHS does not have there. **That is a reading of the pair and not of its reach**, and changing the order rule on the strength of one verse would put fault (1) back for the 87 sentences that set the pair the usual way.

(33) **A notation with no bar in it is not a variant, and the ALEXANDRIAN COLUMN was paying for it.** The same conflation as (30), on the other side of the page and found by looking there on purpose. The module docstring has always distinguished Stipp's two notations — `§ A \ B #` states the reach of the bar, `§ A #` is a marked stretch with no alternative where the two editions have **the same consonants** and the difference is described in the note, a transposition marked with his `*` or a matter of pointing — and `split_variant()` did not. It asked whether a segment was inside *a* notation, so a barless one went to the masoretic column alone and the alexandrian cell emptied. **71 sentences, 809 consonants, 33 of them carrying the star.**

**Nothing this project prints could see it**, which is the point worth keeping. The BHSA check reads the masoretic column, and `common` and `mtvar` both appear there, so the exact-verse count, the recall and the word-for-word figure did not move by one: 1,365 / 0.996 / 1,299 before and after. **A column with no ground truth accumulates faults silently, and (30) stood as long as it did because its twin on the unmeasured side was never looked for.**

Two checks stand in for the one that does not exist. **Stipp's own Greek, sentence by sentence**: it is printed beside 44 of the 71, and at 1,19c the alexandrian cell held nothing while the Greek reads τοῦ ἐξαιρεῖσθαί σε λέγει κύριος, which is לְהַצִּילֶךָ and נְאֻם־יְהוָה in the other order. And **bulk**: masoretic 84,904 consonants against alexandrian 71,973 gave a ratio of 1.180, and the repair brings it to **1.166**, where Janzen's estimate for the short edition puts it near 1.14.

**The test is per NOTATION and not per sentence**, because a sentence can carry two of which only one holds a bar. 41,6a is the single place in the book: its first notation marks the transposition of יִשְׁמָעאֵל לִקְרָאתָם, which the Greek has entire in the other order, and the second is a real variant. **The general invariant is now a floor**: a bar has two sides, so a sentence showing one must show the other. It was true of 1,292 sentences and false of 72; it is now false of **6**, and all six open a notation and hand it to the sentence that follows, where the bar is.

(34) **A bare space swallowed the backslash's BACKWARD reference.** `add()` has guarded the forward one since 2,36a and `bare_slash()` never guarded the other. Stipp sets his apparatus inline and the gap around it arrives as a Hebrew span of pure space; standing between the masoretic word and the bar it left the function looking at spacing, finding no word to mark, and giving up — so the masoretic reading stayed `common` and printed in the ALEXANDRIAN column as well. 13,16f read יָשִׁית יֻשָׁת לַעֲרָפֶל there, where only the second word is alexandrian. **20 sentences, and the invariant is now a floor at zero**: a bar always has its masoretic side in the sentence it stands in, unlike the other direction, where six sentences legitimately hand the notation on.

**A WORD IS NOT A SEGMENT, and that is the half of it that cost two attempts.** The 15th edition emits much of its Hebrew one glyph per span, so the word nearest the bar arrives as several segments of which the last is a single letter or a bare point. Marking that alone cut לֹא יֵאָסְפוּ into לֹא יאָסְפ and a lone waw at 8,2g and left the tsere behind. Collapsing the whole `common` run into one segment instead **cost 85 verses of the masoretic column**, 1,365 → 1,280, because every index already recorded into `segs` — `tight_pairs`, `scope_tight` — shifted under it. What works is to walk the run by the **INNER edges** of its segments, the left edge of the right-hand one against the right edge of the left-hand one, since the column runs right to left while a segment's ASCII does not. **Never reindex `segs` in place; mark, insert, and leave the rest where it is.**

**And a bar with nothing after it in the sentence marks nothing before it.** The backward reference is warranted only once the forward one has been answered: a bar divides two readings, so if the alexandrian side never arrives the parser has found no variant and has no ground for taking the masoretic word out of the shared text. Without that bound 36,1a loses יְהוּדָה from the alexandrian column, where the Greek has βασιλέως Ιουδα. One sentence in the book, and it is the difference between 20 repaired and 20 repaired with 1 broken.

(35) **The abbreviation runs both ways, and the second way is MARKED and not completed.** Where the two forms differ in their FIRST letters Stipp prints the masoretic head and the alexandrian word in full, and (26) finishes the masoretic word from his page — 17 places, each checked against BHSA exactly. Where they differ at the END he does the reverse: the masoretic word entire, and of the alexandrian form only its tail, so the alexandrian column was left holding מַת at 1,18 for וּלְחֹמַת and תָם at 3,18 for אֲבוֹתָם. **39 sentences.** They now carry a dashed underline and a dagger, and the page says what they are.

**They are NOT completed, and the reason is the one that forbids taking letters from BHSA.** The cut is not mechanical: שָׂרָיו against הֶם drops one masoretic letter and אֲבִיכֶם against הֶם drops two, and both tails are two consonants; at 27,11d the tail is a single waw and the masoretic word already opens with one. **And nothing could check the result.** The seventeen completions on the masoretic side were allowed because BHSA verified every one of them; this column has no verifier at all, so a completion here would be a guess written into the text, which is what the removed BHSA repair was. **Marking says the same thing honestly and costs nothing.**

**Which side is the fragment is read off the setting, not the reading.** Tightness already says the bar is inside a word; `_supplied()` says whether the masoretic side can be finished, and if it cannot, the alexandrian side is the fragment — provided it is the shorter piece and neither side closes on a maqqef, which is a word boundary in Stipp's own terms and marks the third class of tight bar, a short whole-word variant. **One further guard, and 7,5b is the only place it matters**: a single letter under a sheva that is not a final form can only OPEN a word, so `אִם \ וְ` is a variant of its own and not the end of the masoretic word — the Greek has καὶ ποιοῦντες. The 2fs suffix at 31,32 is a kaf under a sheva too and IS a tail, which is why the test needs the final form as well as the point. **62 bare tight bars: 17 completed on the masoretic side, 41 marked on the alexandrian side over 39 sentences, 4 left alone.**

**The index page's German note carried transcribed figures and three of them were wrong**, which is the same fault as any other transcribed number in this project. It gave the consonant share as a word share, printed 1.299 where the badge count was 1.365, said a sixth of the verses deviate where it is a fiftieth, and said eight verses were missing when none had been since 2026-09-07. Every figure in it is now computed in `build_synopse_pages.py` at the point of writing.

### The geometry, and where pypdf lies

**X is only mostly trustworthy and Y is unusable across text objects** — but each still says something, and four faults turn on knowing which.

(4) **A printed line is not a run of consecutive spans**: a raised D, P or Q has a y of its own and cuts the line it belongs to in two. (7) **On five lines the x coordinates collapse**, because pypdf reports the origin of the text OBJECT: at 3,6a the whole clause and its label come back at x = 30.2. Such a line has no span at the right edge yet ends in a label-shaped one, and its first Hebrew span marks where the margin stops — 3,6a; 5,17a; 19,6b; 33,11a, and 33,11 had been missing from the book altogether.

(6, 20) **The orphan glyphs are TEXT, and only their position was ever missing.** 718 spans report x = 0.0 exactly — no span anywhere reports an x between 0 and 28 — and 56 of them carry a single Hebrew letter: the 15th edition's one-glyph-per-span setting, where a glyph never received a matrix. Dropping them was right as far as it went, since each otherwise became a line of its own at the head of the page, printed before that page's first verse (the ה at 1,11; 42 of the 181 pages carry one). But they carry **ל ×12, ם ×11, ה ×10, ת ×7, י ×5, ר ×5, ו ×4** — the shape of the END of a Hebrew word — and **57 verses were a letter or two short** because of them: 1,13d lost the ה of רֹאֶה, 7,21 the ל of יִשְׂרָאֵל, 9,6 and 11,22 the ת of צְבָאוֹת. DOCUMENT ORDER survived where the matrix did not: each sits between its line's margin note and that line's first glyph, which is exactly where the leftmost letter of a right-to-left line belongs. `on_page()` gives each the y of the next span inside the frame and an x one glyph to its left, and it rejoins its own line.

(18) **A printed line set as TWO text objects comes back left half first**, and the column runs right to left. **Nothing is ever lost to this — only the order is wrong**, which is why no check that counts letters had ever moved. At 10,16 the label `c` sits on the right half with `וְיִשְׂרָאֵל שֵׁבֶט` while `נַחֲלָתוֹ`, the left half of the same line, went to the clause before, giving `הוּא נַחֲלָתוֹ וְיִשְׂרָאֵל שֵׁבֶט` for BHSA's `הוּא וְיִשְׂרָאֵל שֵׁבֶט נַחֲלָתוֹ`; at 44,1 the same put `וּבְאֶרֶץ פַּתְרוֹס לֵאמֹר` before `הַיֹּשְׁבִים בְּמִגְדֹּל`. The halves are known by geometry: **the second lies entirely to the right of the first**, impossible between two real lines of a right-aligned column. y is only the guard — 133 pairs are of that shape, 127 step down by the ordinary 12.7 and are real successive lines, the rest jump upward out of the coordinate space. `_rejoin_split_lines()` runs BEFORE the lines are grouped into clauses, because the split can fall across a clause boundary, as at 10,16 where the label rides on the far half.

(15, 16, 19) **`MARGIN_X = 170` is a threshold on a column that overflows it.** The text is set flush right and a long line runs past the left edge into the margin zone, where the leading-margin loop swallows it: at 2,34a `נְקִיִּים` sits at x = 141.5 with the bracket closing its plus at 166.1, so the word AND its bracket were lost; at 33,21 the line reaches x = 54. `split_columns()` grows the text leftwards through that run, taking a span when it is within one glyph-width of what follows **or wide enough to reach it** — the width estimated from the glyphs, since pypdf reports only the origin: a base letter advances **5.7** units, a point **3.4**, a space **4.1**. Markup travels with it, because the bracket at 2,34a is part of the line.

**`OVERFLOW_X = 45`, and how it got there is the lesson.** It was first swept to 80, on numbers that fault (18) was corrupting — halves of a printed line counted as extra where they landed. Re-swept after (18), verses exact read 140 → 1,180, 120 → 1,198, 100 → 1,224, 80 → 1,231, 60 → 1,233, **50 / 45 / 40 → 1,236**, 36 → 1,232, 25 → 1,225. The plateau is 40–50 and 45 is the middle of it, which is where a threshold belongs; below 40 the margin lemmas at x = 30–38 start coming in. **A threshold swept against a metric that another fault is corrupting will be set in the wrong place: re-sweeping after each fix is not optional.**

(28) **An apparatus set inside the line took the text after it into the margin.** Stipp sets his parenthetical apparatus inline in the text column, and a long one pushes what follows past the left edge of the column into the margin zone, where the leading-margin loop swallows it — the apparatus is not markup, so the overflow walk stops dead at it. At 2,25a `מִצִּמְאָה` stands beyond `(AlT = וּגְרוֹנֵךְ)` at x = 80 with the apparatus running from 113 to 170, and went with it. **The parentheses say where the printed line begins**: read visually an apparatus is `( … )`, so a `)` in the text column with no `(` before it can only have opened further left, on the same line. **18 verses**, and six verse-end signs that had been read as margin notes came back into the text with them.

**The concession is bounded at both ends, and it has to be.** Reaching for any parenthesis at all puts back two faults this file exists to record. At 8,1 the margin reads `7,30–8,3 > 4Q70* (4QJer` and taking that span into the text makes the `>` a bracket again — fault (17), 89 letters, the largest single loss in the book. At 25,38 it reads `46,16e; 50,16b; Zef 3,1 (` and taking it lets the walk go on to the lemma beyond, which then prints as text. Both are refused by one test: **the `(` must open its span.** An apparatus that stands in the text is set as a parenthesis of its own — at 2,25a the span is `(` and nothing else — while a margin note that happens to contain one carries its cross-reference in front of it. Nothing is taken from further left than `OVERFLOW_X` either, and if either test fails the line is left exactly as it was; there is no partial reach.

(31) **A plus cannot open in the margin and close in the text with its word thrown away.** Stipp brackets a plus that wraps to the left end of a printed line; the closing bracket lands in the text column and the opening bracket and the bracketed word land beyond `MARGIN_X`, so the word was dropped and only its closer survived. At 26,11 the line closes with a `]` at x = 139 and לֵאמֹר stands at x = 40 behind a `[` that shares its span with the cross-reference `7`.

**The test is structural and uses no geometry at all, which is why it goes where `OVERFLOW_X` cannot.** Twelve of these words sit at **x = 30.2**, the leftmost printed position, where the margin's own lemmas also live, so no threshold can separate them — sweeping `OVERFLOW_X` below 40 was measured in (15,16,19) and brings the lemmas in. A bracket can. The text column must OPEN with the closer, and the margin must yield the matching opener at the END of one of its spans — the same positional guard fault (17) needs, since a `>` with a reference after it is Stipp's siglum for *fehlt in* and not a bracket — with Hebrew between the two and nothing but markup or space besides. **19 printed lines of the book are of that shape and every one is a real plus**, which is the whole of the evidence for the rule: it was not tuned, it was counted. **17 verses**, 13 of them the ones the diagnostic filed under `margin` and five it had filed elsewhere (8,3 `misordered`, 15,11 `alexandrian`, 20,5 `parsed`, 29,2 extra text, 38,9 `short`) — a reminder that the diagnostic's categories are a hypothesis and not a measurement.

(32) **A raised glyph between the two halves of a split line keeps them apart.** (18) puts back a printed line set as two text objects by comparing a line with the one after it. A line carrying nothing but a RAISED glyph — a Klammerkonstruktion index at 6.36pt, or a raised Q, P or D — has a y of its own, arrives as a line between the halves, and the pair was then never compared. At 32,35d the index `1` sits at x = 112.9 between the left half of 32,36a and its right half, so the left half stayed where it fell and **the opening formula of 32,36 ran on into the end of 32,35: 28 letters on the wrong verse at each end, the largest single deviation in the book**, and the two halves of it were sitting in the table as one `lost` verse and one `EXTRA` verse without anyone connecting them.

**Only a raised line may be stepped over, and the bound is not optional.** Stepping over ANY line with no Hebrew on it — margin-only lines, apparatus-only lines — costs eleven verses and **loses one from the book altogether**, 1,365 → 1,350 and 1,364 verses → 1,363. The discriminator is the font size the source itself uses, 6.36pt against the body's 9.88pt, which is the same measurement (5,23) needed for the Klammerkonstruktion index.

(36) **The text column can close a bracket it never opened ANYWHERE in its run, not only in its first span.** (31) asked the question of the head of the column, which is where the closer stands when the bracketed plus is the whole of a wrapped line. It need not be. At 52,29 Stipp brackets the entire line, so the opener and שְׁלֹשִׁים וּשְׁנַיִם stand at the far left and the closer at the far right, past the text; at 34,10 the closer comes after the column's first Hebrew rather than before it. **The test is the one the file already applies to a parenthesis** — `_apparatus_opens_left()` asks whether a run of spans closes something it never opened, and `_bracket_closes_left()` now asks it of the brackets. **3 verses.**

(37) **Two things belong to the printed line and are not markup, so the leftward walk stopped dead at them.** The walk carries a bracket along because a bracket is markup; it breaks at anything else that is not Hebrew. **An apparatus complete inside its own span** is the first — Stipp's `(!)` — and (28) cannot reach it, because (28) fires on a `)` that opens no parenthesis and this one closes its own. At 7,8 it stands at x = 128 with הוֹעִיל beyond it at 97. **The transposition star** is the second: at 14,17 it stands at x = 137 between the column and וְיוֹמָם. **2 verses**, and 10,7 came with them.

Together **1,365 → 1,370**, word for word 1,299 → 1,303, recall 0.996 → 0.997, no losses, and the margin class falls **7 → 3**. What is left of the masoretic column is 9 verses missing letters and 16 carrying letters BHSA does not have, in five or six separate small causes with no shared mechanism between them. **That is where to stop chasing it**; the remaining faults of the OTHER two columns are described under (33) to (35) and are larger.

(38) **A word broken in two on these pages was never the source's typesetting, and pypdf was the reason it looked like it.** 41 spaces inside a Hebrew word — הַדָּבָ ר five times in the formula of the C superscriptions, בִּשְׁלֹ שׁ at 1,2, שְׁלֹ שִׁים, יֹאמֵר וּ — were taken for justification gaps the source sets, and left. They have two causes, and both are visible in the PDF itself to PyMuPDF, which reads glyph by glyph with real boxes:

- **pypdf decides where a space goes.** The 15th edition sets much of its Hebrew one glyph per `Tj`, and pypdf joins those into a string with a space wherever the jump between two glyphs looks large to it. At 21,1 the glyphs of הדבר touch to within 0.07 units. **27 places.**
- **The source has a hair space.** At 1,2 the PDF does carry a space glyph inside בשלש, set at **1.78pt and 0.2 units wide**. Of the book's Hebrew space glyphs, 17,284 are set at the body's 12.72pt and are 4.1 wide; 16 are set below 5pt. **14 places.**

`_true_spaces()` aligns the two readings BY INDEX, which is possible because over all 181 pages pypdf's Hebrew and PyMuPDF's **fill pass** are the same 172,779 non-space glyphs in the same order. The fill pass matters: each glyph of a faked bold is drawn twice, fill then stroke (`type` 1 in `get_texttrace()`, 5,652 glyphs), and pypdf reports only one. A whitespace run is dropped only if the trace has a hair space there, or no space glyph at all with both glyphs on one baseline and touching; the **629** that fall between lines and the **32** with a clear gap on one line are kept. Nothing is added and no glyph moves. Word for word 1,303 → **1,330**; exact 1,370 → **1,371**, because 11,18's false space had cut עֵנִי in two and its yod was being read as common text; and four sentences got back the vowel points the space had parted from their letter (הָאֵזוֹר 13,11; דִּבְרֵי 36,10; יְהוֹיָקִים 22,18; וְגַם 26,20).

**What the trace cannot do is put a space back.** Within one pypdf string the gaps are bimodal — 0 inside a word, at least 2.5 between — with nothing between 0.5 and 2.0, and pypdf already spaces every one of them. At a SEAM between two strings it is not so: a gap of 3.3 turns up inside words as well (הַ + מַּמְלָכוֹת), most likely where a bracket set in the Times font stands outside the Hebrew trace. So the ten glued pairs stay, and 50,40 is the one checked against a rendered crop: the page shows a narrow gap between יְהוָה and לֹא on a squeezed line, and the PDF draws it as a jump of 2.4 units with no space glyph.

**The pin that held the old reading was a test of the fault.** `test_the_justification_gaps_are_the_source_s_own` asserted that 1,2 prints בשל and ש; and the test for (36) looked for שים at 52,29, the half of שלשים that the false space left standing as a word. A test written against the page as it is will pin whatever is wrong with it.

(39) **The margin was printed as a heap of pieces, and the footnotes as margin.** No figure could see either, since the BHSA check reads the text column. Found by putting a rendered crop of the PDF beside 1,2, where Stipp sets two margin lines, `→ 1a` and `25,3a₁; 46,25b`, and the page said `1a → ; 46,25b 25,3a`.

- **`split_columns()`'s lookahead hands the LAST piece of every margin line to the text column.** A span stays in the margin only while the span after it is also left of `MARGIN_X`, which protects the lines whose x collapses, and the price was that the last margin piece went to the text, was filed as `sigla`, and was printed before the rest. The column split is NOT changed, because it decides the Hebrew. Instead a non-Hebrew piece left of `MARGIN_X` on a line that has not collapsed is treated as margin wherever it landed, and `_margin_lines()` joins each printed line's pieces left to right into one note, with a raised index (6.36pt) rejoining the line nearest in y as a subscript or superscript digit. **The spaces are the PDF's own**: a span carries the space after it, and Joël is three spans, so a joiner that adds spaces prints `Jo ë l`. Position decides only where no span supplies one (`W_APP = 4.0`), and a change of script always gets a space. The margin's own parentheses, which the loop consumed as apparatus markup, are now printed — 102 `(` and 89 `)` — except a `(` that ends the whole line, which opens the text column's apparatus (`| (` before τοῦ θεοῦ at 1,1a) because pypdf hands back such a line as one Times span from x = 30. **Audit: the three columns are byte-identical and the notes hold exactly the same characters as before, plus the parentheses.** Mixed notes are rendered left to right with each Hebrew run isolated (`heb_runs()`); a whole-note `direction:rtl` would reverse the German references after a lemma.
- **The 23 footnotes were read as the margin of the page's last sentence**, in pieces: footnote 1 came out as nine notes with `M` + `ICHAEL` + `L` + `ANGLOIS` in four of them. They are set at 8.48pt with small capitals at 6.70 and the number at 5.64, sizes nothing else in the book uses for text, and the Hebrew they quote at 11.32. `_take_footnotes()` removes each from pypdf's stream as one run, number to last word, and `footnotes()` reads it whole with PyMuPDF, line by line. **A footnote goes with the sentence that carries its mark**, and the mark is told from a Klammerkonstruktion index, which has the same size, by DIRECTION: every footnote mark of the book is 4.5–4.6 units ABOVE its line, and every index is 0 to 1.5 below. Three marks (17, 18, 19) are in the Greek panel, where `greek_clauses()` had been reading any digits as the next Greek verse number; they are now skipped there and the footnote goes to the row that received that Greek. Footnotes 18 and 19 had stood on the wrong verse (27,10c for 27,6a; 36,28e for 36,24a).
- **The footnotes had leaked into the text, and that is the half that moved a figure.** The Hebrew a footnote quotes stood in the masoretic column after the sof pasuq — שׁמם at 4,17, בָּהּכֹּה at 9,18, כי at 15,14, חֲצַר הַמַּטָּרָה at 37,21 — and footnote 19's Greek, ἐξέστησαν ἐξ ἐζήτησαν, stood as Stipp's Greek at 36,28e. Exact 1,371 → **1,375**, word for word 1,330 → **1,334**, extra-text ceiling 15 → **11**. At 37,21 the length-based Greek placement moved ἕως ἐξέλιπον οἱ ἄρτοι τῆς πόλεως to sentence c, because sentence d's Hebrew was no longer inflated.

**(40)–(42) came out of the first case-by-case review, 2026-10-01**, the 13 word-division cases left after (38), each read against a rendered crop of the PDF (`page.get_pixmap(clip=...)`). Four mechanisms, none of them a typesetting accident:

(40) **Taking the other edition's text out must not join two words.** `column()` drops what a column does not have, and the space that went with it was sometimes the only junction between two kept words. Two shapes: a plus that STRADDLES the boundary — `נִכְתָּם<תּ בְּ>עֲוֹנֵךְ` at 2,22, the Greek's נכתמת בעונך, and `שַׁעֲטַת<וֹ מִ>פַּרְסוֹת` at 47,3 — and the space that followed an alexandrian-only reading, `חֶרְפַּת\ה <מִ>נְעוּרַי` at 31,19, where it stood between two removed pieces. (42) **A word space drawn as a gap.** `_true_spaces()` now also ADDS a space, in one case: two pypdf strings meeting with no space in either, a gap of 2 units or more between the glyphs on one baseline, and **no glyph of any font standing in the gap**. ### Step 4: positions from PyMuPDF (2026-10-01)

**Every span's position now comes from PyMuPDF; its text and its boundaries are still pypdf's.** `_true_geometry()` gives each span the origin of its first glyph — the alignment by index that (38) found holds for the non-Hebrew glyphs too, on all 181 pages, once the stroke pass is set aside — with y as page height less baseline, pypdf's own convention wherever pypdf is right. The span boundaries stay because dozens of reading rules are written against them. (A switch, `SYNOPSE_GEOMETRY=pypdf`, kept the old geometry for comparison during the migration; it was removed in the clean-up below.)

**The change that made it work is the order of lines, not the coordinates.** With true y, `_lines_by_baseline()` orders lines top to bottom, which the pypdf reading could not do (its y was not comparable across text objects), so it kept DOCUMENT order and repaired it in `_rejoin_split_lines()`. Ordered by height, exact went 1,209 → 1,382 at a stroke and the rejoin (18, 32) stopped mattering — measured with it and without it, the same. Correcting coordinates while keeping document order had been tried as (47) and failed for exactly that reason.

Each further adjustment was found by listing the verses the new geometry lost against the old and reading one; all are recorded at the code:

- **A small glyph belongs to its line**: an index, a raised P/D/Q or a footnote mark joins the full-size line within 6 units. Without it a₁/a₂ came back as a, and 172 records opened with no label. Only a full-size span WITH TEXT founds a line — a whitespace span placed after an index founded one of its own and captured it (25,29 a₂).
- **A small digit alone is never a label**: a long margin line reached the label column with the subscript of `24,8a₂` at 1,15.
- **A whitespace span stands after the glyph BEFORE it**, of either font; placed at the next glyph of its own font it crossed a Times bar and the bar stopped being tight (3,23, 5,18, 6,8 lost their completed word).
- **x includes a span's leading space**, as pypdf's does; every distance threshold was tuned on that (7,8).
- **The walk steps over a raised siglum**, which now rides on its line (2,13).

**What was tried and failed, recorded so it is not tried again.** A Hebrew–Greek lexicon induced from the verse-level pairing (Dice over 1,297 verses, 4,381 pairs, and it does learn real equivalences — `וראו/ιδετε`, `חטאנו/ημαρτομεν`, `עשיתי/εποιησα`) scores **worse** than dealing in order, 61 against 47; its best over an eighteen-point sweep reached 44, which is selecting on the test. Added on top of the length score it changes nothing at any weight from 0.05 to 0.5 and is worse above it. Length alone, with no parameter to tune, gives 7.

(29) **One page of the 181 is not a page of the text.** p. 141 is headed *Jer 43,3–9 mit 4Q72a (4QJer d)* and sets a passage the book has already given, this time beside the Qumran scroll. **(45) was tried and taken out: putting back the head of a line pypdf reports elsewhere.** 36,1b and 29,10c are one fault, not two. After a BOLD idiolect reference (`Id 2.5`, `Id 4.10`) the PDF shifts the origin, and pypdf returns the HEAD of that printed line — the reference, the margin, and the first sign of the text — at y ≈ −4300 while the rest of the line is at its normal y. The bar of 36,1b and the closing `[` of 29,10c sit in those heads, at the RIGHT x. (46) **The overflow walk steps over Stipp's stroke `|`**, as (37) taught it to step over an apparatus and the star. **(47) was tried and taken out: the true x of every span.** Step 1 aligned pypdf's and PyMuPDF's Hebrew glyph by glyph, and **the non-Hebrew glyphs align the same way on all 181 pages**, so every span's true x is available from its first glyph's ORIGIN (not its box, which starts elsewhere for a point: comparing boxes moved 3,644 spans). Three measured versions: correcting the Hebrew alone, per line, skipping lines that collapse entirely, rose to exact 1,385 but left brackets at the collapsed x and words at the true one, so the two read in the wrong order — 31,12c's alexandrian column lost תִּירֹשׁ and eight verse-end signs slipped into a plus; correcting every span moved 6,340 and fell to 1,370. **The reason is general and is the case for step 4**: `MARGIN_X`, `OVERFLOW_X` and the lookahead were tuned against pypdf's WRONG coordinates, so correcting coordinates in bulk invalidates the thresholds that compensate for them. Moving to PyMuPDF is a re-sweep of every threshold against the whole suite, not a patch. Two traps met on the way and worth keeping: a table keyed on `id()` must be cleared per page, because Python reuses a freed tuple's id — not clearing it cost 270 verses; and a leading space is one space-width in pypdf's x and none in the glyph's.
